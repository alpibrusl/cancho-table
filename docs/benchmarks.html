Benchmarks

Supporting evidence for the speed claims on the overview. How long table, csvtk, Miller and DuckDB take to answer the same questions, on two machines. Lower is better. All times are seconds unless a unit is given.

How these were measured

  • The file. 1,000,000 rows of id,status,bytes,path,note (the last field quoted), 31,667,311 bytes. status has a few distinct values; bytes is a whole number.
  • The timing. The best of five runs, taken one tool after another so a busy machine affects all of them. Output goes to /dev/null. Memory is the peak, from /usr/bin/time.
  • Answers first. Each tool’s answer is compared with one computed in Python before it is timed. A tool that disagrees is reported, not timed.
  • The tools. csvtk 0.38.0 on one core. Miller 6.22.0 (Linux only). DuckDB 1.5.6 (Mac only), reading with read_csv and type detection, writing to /dev/null. awk and cut | sort | uniq -c are shown where they apply as a floor: they do not understand CSV quoting.
  • Cores. A core is one independent worker in a processor. table --threads N uses N; the default is 1. DuckDB uses all by default.
  • Machines. Mac: Apple silicon, 16 cores. Linux: x86-64, 6 cores (three physical, each running two) of a shared box, with other work running.

Linux, with DuckDB

The same questions as the Mac, on the Linux machine, now with DuckDB 1.5.6 (the Mac’s version). The machine is an i7-1260P: six hardware threads on three physical cores, shared with other users’ work (load 2 to 5 during these runs, and a short burst of 24 that coincided with cells in which csvtk used 14.5 GB of memory). Every tool was pinned to those six threads, at the lowest priority. table is shown on 1 and 6 threads, DuckDB on 1 and 6 threads (SET threads=6; DuckDB’s own default would be 16 here, more than the cores it may use). Seconds, the minimum of 5 runs (3 for the 1 GB file), output to /dev/null, every answer checked against Python first. “table against DuckDB” says who is faster and by how much, at 1 thread and at 6 threads. Because six threads are only three cores, both tools gain less from threads than on the Mac.

  • Scans. On the four everyday questions table is 1.6 to 3.1 times faster than DuckDB at one thread and 2.3 to 2.8 times at six. table on one thread is level with or ahead of DuckDB on six.
  • Where DuckDB is ahead. Groupings with many keys (1.1 to 1.9 times at 100,000 keys, 4 to 11 times at a million), distinct over unique values, sorting whole files (2.0 to 3.1 times at six threads; table sorts on one core), and the 17-digit number column (min and max 4.8 times slower at one thread, 2.9 times at six; the exact reader scales on Linux). The group by a number in two spellings is 1.4 to 1.7 times slower than DuckDB here, where on the Mac it was level at one thread.
  • The type report is a full read: 6.4 times slower than DuckDB’s sampling DESCRIBE at one thread and 3 times at four or six; 1.7 times faster than SUMMARIZE at one thread and 3.6 times at four.
  • Exactness. The same as on the Mac: table gave the exact float sum in every group at every thread count, DuckDB DOUBLE in none (once 1 of 4 groups at four threads, 17-digit column), and its answer changed with the thread count in every one of the six questions (3 distinct answers over 1, 4 and 6 threads).

Good to know

  • Many distinct keys are the weak spot. Each new key is a new group, and the groups are sorted at the end. More cores do not help, and use more memory (a million groups took 135 to 183 MB, with the group limit raised to 1 GiB). Do not use --threads for a group-by like that.
  • Long fields with newlines inside do not speed up with more cores. The same file without newlines in the fields goes from 0.017 s to 0.0086 s on eight cores; with a newline every 17 bytes it stays at 0.17 s.
  • Memory. A few MB for a scan. csvtk’s summary used 11.8 to 14.8 GB on the 100,000- and 1,000,000-key sums, and Miller 0.5 to 3.9 GB.

Rerun them

Build build/table as the overview shows, then, from the repository root:

python3 scripts/bench.py                 # the 1,000,000-row file; every answer checked, then timed
python3 scripts/bench_parallel.py        # the same questions on 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 cores
python3 scripts/adversarial.py           # the harder cases; --cells B1,B3 --runs 5 --threads 8

Files are generated under build/ and are never committed. csvtk, Miller and DuckDB are used where they are installed. The numbers come from the documents in the repository: harder cases, cores, DuckDB comparison.
